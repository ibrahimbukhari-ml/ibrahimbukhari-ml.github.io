    Paris · Mar – Aug 2025

    AI Research Intern at OHVaiLLM.

    Aligning financial LLMs for reliability and traceability in RAG: post-training them on synthetic data so they cite the exact sentence behind every claim, and hallucinate less. This work was also my MSc thesis.

    01 · Sources

    Financial answers need receipts.

    Financial reports are long, dense and full of numbers. An assistant answering questions about them should point to the exact sentence each claim comes from.

    The problem: answers that sound right but can't be traced back to the source.

    02 · Agents

    A pipeline of LLM agents.

    A multi-agent pipeline reads the source documents, writes question–answer pairs and checks them. Samples that fail a check are dropped before they reach the dataset.

    The agents shown are illustrative.

    03 · Dataset

    40,000+ samples, every claim cited.

    Every claim in every answer is linked to the exact source sentence that supports it. The threads are those citations, and producing them is what the model learns.

    Also built: FinBench-Cite, a benchmark for sentence-level citation.

    04 · Training

    Post-training at scale.

    LLMs were post-trained on the dataset to cite at sentence level in RAG, on multiple H100 GPUs of the Jean Zay supercomputer, orchestrated with Slurm.

    Hugging FacevLLMFAISSSlurm

    05 · Results

    Fewer hallucinations, better citations.

    After post-training, hallucinations dropped by 17 points and citation accuracy rose by 48 points.

    Reading the chart: left bar, hallucination (down 17 points); right bar, citation accuracy (up 48 points). Drawn to scale.

    Donostia · Jun – Aug 2024

    NLP Research Intern at Vicomtech.

    Synthetic data for few-shot intent and entity recognition in task-specific dialogue systems, where only a handful of labelled examples exist per intent.

    PythonDistilBERTJointBERTTransformersTensorFlow

    Scroll for the story ↓

    01 · Seeds

    A handful of examples.

    A task-specific dialogue system starts with very few labelled utterances per intent. Each one pairs an intent with its entities, highlighted here.

    The problem: recognising intents and entities with almost no training data.

    02 · Variants

    Same entities, new contexts.

    An LLM generates new utterances from each seed's intent–entity pair by substituting the context around the entity. The entity stays in place; the words around it change.

    03 · Filter

    Catching mislabeled samples.

    Some generated utterances no longer match their intent. A DistilBERT classifier trained on the seed data flags them, and the flagged ones are dropped before training.

    Effect: 40% less mislabeling, and 3 points more overall accuracy.

    Not every bad sample is caught: the outlined ones made it through.

    04 · Evaluation

    One model, two answers.

    The clean data goes into a JointBERT-based evaluation: one model predicts the intent and tags the entities in the same pass, measured by intent accuracy and entity-level F1.

    05 · Results

    Entity F1 up by 30 points.

    Training on the synthetic data improved entity F1 by 30 points, and filtering out mislabeled samples added 3 points of overall accuracy.

    Reading the chart: left bar, entity F1 (up 30 points); right bar, overall accuracy from filtering (up 3 points). Drawn to scale.
