PhD Research

Visual Language Models for Inclusion.

A four-year PhD on how language models can see, follow and faithfully translate sign language, and on what that teaches us about vision-language models in general.

The question

Can a language model use what it sees over time?

I study how video is encoded, how those representations are aligned with the language model, and whether the output stays grounded in the visual evidence. I approach these questions through sign language understanding and translation, and I'm interested in building open, spatio-temporally aligned sign language data along the way.

  • Encoding: representations that keep fine detail and global structure.
  • Time: long-range temporal dependencies in signing.
  • Alignment: how visual tokens reach the language model.
  • Grounding: outputs tied to visual evidence, not the model's prior.

01 · Signing

Meaning starts as motion.

Sign languages carry meaning through the hands, face and body at once, in space and over time. To a model, all of it arrives as a stream of pixels.

Why it's hard: parallel channels, fine detail and very little annotated data.

02 · Encoding

From pixels to structure.

A vision encoder compresses video into something a language model can use (joints, handshapes, movement) without losing the fine detail that tells one sign from another.

Question: how should signing video be encoded?

03 · Time

Meaning unfolds over time.

A single frame is rarely enough. Grammar lives in movement and in the signing space, so the representation has to follow how the hands travel across many frames.

Question: how do we model long-range temporal dependencies?

04 · Alignment

Into the language model.

Each stretch of motion becomes a visual token, projected into the space where the language model reasons. How well those tokens are aligned decides what the model can actually use.

Question: how are visual representations aligned with the LLM?

05 · Grounding

Words tied to evidence.

A faithful translation traces every word back to what was signed, not to what the language model finds likely. The lines show which visual tokens each word draws on.

Question: does the output stay grounded in the visual evidence?

Illustrative output, not a real model prediction.
